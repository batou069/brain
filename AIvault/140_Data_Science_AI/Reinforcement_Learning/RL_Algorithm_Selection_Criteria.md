# RL: Criteria for Choosing an RL Algorithm

Choosing the right Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithm for a specific problem is a critical decision that significantly impacts the success and efficiency of your project. There is no single "best" algorithm; the optimal choice depends on the characteristics of your [[RL_Environment|environment]] and your problem.

>[!question]- What are the criteria for choosing an RL algorithm?

Here are the key criteria to consider when selecting an RL algorithm:

[list2tab|#RL Algorithm Selection Criteria]
- 1. State Space Type
    -   **Discrete State Space:**
        -   **Description:** The number of possible states is finite and manageable (e.g., grid cells in a small maze, positions in a simple board game).
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Tabular methods (e.g., [[RL_Q_Learning|Q-Learning]], [[RL_SARSA|SARSA]], [[RL_Monte_Carlo_Methods|Monte Carlo Control]]) are often effective and guaranteed to converge.
    -   **Large/Continuous State Space:**
        -   **Description:** The number of states is too large to fit in a table (e.g., raw pixel data from an image, joint angles of a robot).
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Requires [[RL_Deep_Reinforcement_Learning|Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)]] algorithms that use neural networks as function approximators (e.g., DQN, Policy Gradient methods, Actor-Critic methods).
- 2. Action Space Type
    -   **Discrete Action Space:**
        -   **Description:** A finite set of distinct actions (e.g., "move left," "jump," "buy," "sell").
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Value-based methods (e.g., Q-Learning, DQN) and Policy Gradient methods can handle this.
    -   **Continuous Action Space:**
        -   **Description:** Actions are chosen from a continuous range (e.g., "steering angle," "motor torque").
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Value-based methods are generally not suitable. Requires Policy Gradient methods (e.g., DDPG, SAC, PPO) or Actor-Critic methods.
- 3. Environment Dynamics (Model-Based vs. Model-Free)
    -   **Known/Learnable Model (Model-Based RL):**
        -   **Description:** You have access to a simulator of the environment, or the environment's transition probabilities and reward function are known or can be learned.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** [[RL_Model_Based_vs_Model_Free|Model-based RL algorithms]] (e.g., Dyna-Q, [[RL_Monte_Carlo_Tree_Search|MCTS]]) can be very sample efficient.
    -   **Unknown/Complex Model (Model-Free RL):**
        -   **Description:** You only learn through direct interaction with the environment; its dynamics are unknown or too complex to model explicitly.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** [[RL_Model_Based_vs_Model_Free|Model-free RL algorithms]] (e.g., Q-Learning, SARSA, Policy Gradient methods) are necessary.
- 4. Policy Type (On-Policy vs. Off-Policy)
    -   **On-Policy Learning:**
        -   **Description:** The agent learns about the optimal policy by evaluating or improving the *same policy* it uses to act in the environment.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** [[RL_SARSA|SARSA]], Actor-Critic methods (often).
        -   **Consideration:** Can be more stable but less sample efficient.
    -   **Off-Policy Learning:**
        -   **Description:** The agent learns about the optimal policy (target policy) from data generated by a *different* policy (behavior policy).
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** [[RL_Q_Learning|Q-Learning]], DQN, DDPG.
        -   **Consideration:** Can reuse past experience (experience replay) more effectively, leading to better sample efficiency, but can be harder to stabilize.
- 5. Reward Sparsity and Delay
    -   **Dense/Immediate Rewards:**
        -   **Description:** The agent receives frequent and informative reward signals.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Most RL algorithms can work well.
    -   **Sparse/Delayed Rewards:**
        -   **Description:** Positive rewards are rare and only occur after a long sequence of actions.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Requires specialized techniques like [[RL_Hindsight_Experience_Replay_HER|Hindsight Experience Replay (HER)]], [[RL_Curiosity_Regret|curiosity-driven exploration]], reward shaping, or [[RL_Monte_Carlo_Tree_Search|MCTS]].
- 6. Sample Efficiency
    -   **High Sample Efficiency Required:**
        -   **Description:** Interacting with the real environment is expensive, time-consuming, or dangerous (e.g., robotics).
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Model-based RL, off-policy methods with [[RL_Experience_Memory|experience replay]] (e.g., DQN, DDPG, SAC), HER.
    -   **Low Sample Efficiency Tolerated:**
        -   **Description:** Interaction is cheap (e.g., fast simulator, video games).
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** On-policy methods (e.g., PPO, A2C) can be effective.
- 7. Computational Resources
    -   **Limited Resources:**
        -   **Description:** No access to powerful GPUs/TPUs.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Tabular methods (for small state spaces), simpler DRL architectures, or algorithms optimized for CPU.
    -   **Ample Resources:**
        -   **Description:** Access to GPUs/TPUs.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Complex DRL algorithms with large neural networks.
- 8. Interpretability Needs
    -   **High Interpretability Required:**
        -   **Description:** Need to understand *why* the agent makes decisions (e.g., safety-critical systems).
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Simpler models, or DRL with explainable AI (XAI) techniques.
    -   **Low Interpretability Required:**
        -   **Description:** Focus is purely on performance.
        -   **Algorithm Choice:** Complex DRL models (e.g., deep Q-networks, advanced policy gradient methods).

By carefully considering these criteria, you can narrow down the vast landscape of RL algorithms to a few promising candidates for your specific problem.
